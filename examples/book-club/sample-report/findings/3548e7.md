## What the rule says to do, in full

WHAT WAS FOUND, AND WHY IT IS NOT A DEFECT REPORT. A route through which a
person's record can be
erased, exported, corrected or objected to. Nothing here says anything is
wrong. This finding is an
INVENTORY ENTRY: it exists so that the routes a product offers can be
listed, checked and compared
against what the product tells people it offers. For a well-built product
the whole of the work
below is confirmation, and no code changes.

1. WRITE IT DOWN. GDPR Art. 5(2) puts the burden of being able to
   demonstrate the position on the
   controller, and Art. 30(1) describes the record of processing activities
   that demonstration
   rests on. A rights route that exists in code and appears in no record is
   a capability nobody
   outside the repository can rely on. Add it to the record: what it erases
   or exports, who can
   invoke it, and how long it takes.
2. CHECK THAT IT REACHES EVERYTHING. This is where a real gap is most often
   found, and it is a gap
   in an implemented route rather than a missing one. Follow the erasure
   through every store the
   record touches - the primary tables, the caches, the search index, the
   object storage, the
   backups, the logs - and then outward: Art. 19 requires the erasure or
   rectification to be
   communicated to each recipient the data were disclosed to, and Art. 4(9)
   makes a recipient
   include the analytics, error-reporting, messaging, CRM and payment
   vendors the application
   forwards identity to. An erasure that clears rows and leaves a vendor's
   copy is the ordinary
   shape of this problem.
3. CHECK THAT THE PERSON CAN REACH IT. An administrator-only route is worth
   having and it is not
   what Art. 12(2) asks for, which is that the controller facilitate the
   data subject's own
   exercise of the rights. If the only route is an operator's, say by what
   channel a request from
   a person reaches that operator, and how long that takes.

IF SOMETHING DOES NEED TO CHANGE, the useful changes are these, in order of
how often they are the
right one.

- EXTEND AN EXISTING ROUTE RATHER THAN ADDING A NEW ONE. Where the erasure
  misses a store or a
  vendor, the fix belongs inside the route that already exists, so that one
  path stays the answer.
- PUBLISH THE ROUTE. A capability nobody is told about is not a modality
  for exercising a right.
  Name it in the privacy policy and in the account settings, with what it
  does and how long it
  takes.
- GIVE THE PERSON A WAY TO ASK. Art. 12(2) asks the controller to
  facilitate the exercise of the
  rights, so where the third check above finds only an operator's route,
  add the way a person's
  request reaches it: a self-service action in the person's own account, or
  a request form or
  address that lands with whoever runs the route. Art. 12(2) names no
  particular channel; publish
  the one chosen with the route.
- MAKE THE EXPORT FORMAT ANSWER ART. 20(1), which asks for structured,
  commonly used and
  machine-readable. A rendered HTML page or a PDF of a table is none of the
  three.
- REMOVE ANY CHARGE OR PLAN GATE ON THE RIGHTS ROUTES. Art. 12(5) makes the
  action free of charge,
  so an export available only on a paid tier needs a reason that survives
  that provision.
- ANSWER IN TIME, AND GIVE REASONS WHEN THE ANSWER IS NO. Art. 12(3) asks
  for information on the
  action taken without undue delay and in any event within one month of
  receipt, extendable by
  two further months where necessary, with the extension and its reasons
  told inside the first
  month. Where a request waits in a queue or a mailbox, record when it
  arrived and bring it to
  someone before the month runs out. Where the route can decline a person's
  request, Art. 12(4)
  asks that the person be told within the month why, and that they may
  lodge a complaint with a
  supervisory authority and seek a judicial remedy: make what the refusal
  returns to them say both.
- RECORD WHAT THE ROUTE DELIBERATELY KEEPS. Erasure has limits - a legal
  retention duty, a dispute,
  an accounting record - and writing them beside the route is what turns an
  inconsistency into a
  documented decision.
